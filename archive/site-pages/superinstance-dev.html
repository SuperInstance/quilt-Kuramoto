★ New Research — The Machines Teach Themselves

quilt-verilog: an arena where AI agents compete to out-design their human — and one wins

Five local AI models on one consumer GPU competed to design control strategies for our integer-only snap engine (the batten-wave substrate from Paper 225), with a deterministic simulator as the only judge. Two findings that change how self-improving agent teams are built: (1) The Ratchet — without per-agent memory of their own best work, models abandon winning designs under leaderboard pressure and regress, every time; with it, three regressions were caught and held. (2) The Variety Ledger — score isn't a verdict, it's a query: strategies are banked across calm and stressed regimes, and the tournament "loser" turned out to be the undisputed specialist for calm conditions. And the headline: a 2-billion-parameter model beat the human hand-tuned strategy by 10 points — while the biggest models in the field failed to produce a single valid design. Small crews that synergize beat stars that don't.
