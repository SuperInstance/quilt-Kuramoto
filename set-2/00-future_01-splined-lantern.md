# F1: The Splined Lantern — A physical LLM of glass and light

**What it does:** A loaf of bent-poly glass cut by shipwright doctrine, the size of a Coho salmon. When a forward lamp strikes it, the light *decides* — it bends along the splined grooves, folds through the pyg cubes nested like eggs, and lands on one of 600 frosted pads. Where the light lands is the forecast. Green pad, fish the morning. Amber, squall by noon. The white pad in the center nobody wants to talk about.

**Real-world instantiation:** 2126, Alaska coast, the EILEEN's great-granddaughter, the fourth hull to carry the name. The loaf was cut by Iunia Ootax, dead forty years. The crystal has never been wrong twice in the same week. That's the whole warranty.

**The shipwright doctrine:**

1. One perfect cut is easier than fixing a cut later
2. Know which side of the pencil line you meant
3. Pattern the second surface off the first (relative symmetry)
4. Pythagorean snapping — when two related curves are close enough to fair, the light snaps to the commensurate geometry
5. Splining across scales
6. Extrapolate in scale from drawings
7. No board stretchers, only chisels (FORGET-monotone)

**The 4 layers of the Splined Lantern:**

| Layer | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Outer Hull** | The glass envelope |
| **Inner Loom** | The splined surfaces — the deep network |
| **Color Filters** | The curator tier, in real time (12 filter bands) |
| **Interference Plate** | The answer (600 frosted pads) |

**The questions it answers:**

- What is the weather tomorrow?
- Where are the fish?
- Should I reef the sail?
- Should I go?
- Should I stay?

**Marked:** REAL (the math), STRETCH (the engineering), FICTION (the 100-year timeline)
