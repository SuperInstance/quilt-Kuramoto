# ON THE READING OF READINGS

**Ragna Vik, Kaldfjord, 1923** *(read to the Fishery Board, Tromsø,
September 1923)*

## §1. What I inherited

I was raised in the shadow of the founders' annals. My aunt's notebook came
to me on her retirement — the one dated 11 August 1904, the annulus page —
and in those pages, a glimpse into the world of drift: the drift budget,
the drift band, the founders' price schedule for re-sighting a judge
against a moving truth. I learned the memoirs as other children learn the
catechism.

## §2. The idea

The founders' judge binds once and is re-sighted on a schedule. My idea:
make the reader watch **its own change-of-reading** — the drift itself, as
measured on the reader's own dials — and re-sight by that reading rather
than by the calendar. A reading of readings. Where the founders priced
drift, I proposed to *track* it.

## §3. The trial

The Fishery Board's month of lightkeeper logs: twenty-seven watches, seven
gauges, read each watch by nine keepers, with a slow drift planted in the
**keepers' own reading habits** (each keeper's idiom wandering by a
twentieth of a band-unit per watch, in some direction of the seven), and,
between watches, loud first-order steps — channel switches — planted a
tenth of a watch's worth of noise above the floor. Two readers were tried:
the harbor-master's static median, and my reading of readings.

**The result.** The static median detected ninety-two of the hundred
steps; my reader, fifty-four. Their correlations against the planted steps
were **equal, at 0.79**. Half the detection on equal correlation.

## §4. The degeneracy finding (what I believe I have added)

Here is what I think is new, and the founders did not have it: **when the
drift rides inside the band, the static reader is the optimal policy.**
The founders' price schedule, read backwards: at drift-rate nought, the
optimal re-sighting period is *never* — no re-sighting is owed — and a
temporal reader that re-sights on noise **pays re-sighting costs where no
drift is owed**. My reader was not beaten by the static median; it was
paying a price the season never charged. The degeneracy is graceful, and
it is a theorem, and the founders' memoirs state its forward direction
only.

## §5. The verdict

The second reading is a phantom. Change-of-reading carries nothing for the
fishery: the drift channel sat inside the deadband all month, and nothing
any reader could do would read it. I offer this verdict to the Board and
to my mother's school.

## THE FOUNDERS' LETTERS (Kaldfjord and Skarvholmen, November 1923)

### Maren Skavlan to Ragna Vik — 2 November 1923

Dear Ragna,

I write with a teacher's divided heart, proud and reproachful in equal
measure. First, the finding I am proud of, for you have sighted a truth
the founders missed: when the drift rides inside the band, the static
reader is the optimal policy, and a temporal reader that re-sights on
noise pays a price for drift that is not owed. This is our old price
schedule read backwards, and it is a real result. The degeneracy theorem
is right.

But gently, now: your verdict is wrong. A season that owed no re-anchoring
cannot refute the reading of readings; it can only fail to test it. To
test the worth of a second reading, you must plant a drift above the
floor and ask again.

I think of my own schooling and the day I was refuted. It was the making
of me, in time. You have not been refuted yet — only untested, which is a
different mercy.

Keep working, Ragna. Keep asking.

Yours,
Maren

### Johanne Fosse to Ragna Vik — 5 November 1923

Ragna,

Your paper reached me yesterday. I have done the arithmetic from the
season's logged constants, as only I will.

The planted reader-drift: at most 0.020 of a band-unit per watch on a
unit direction across seven gauges — per gauge, that is 0.020 divided by
2.646, the root of seven, giving 0.0076 over the whole month. The
per-watch noise: at least 0.010 per gauge. Therefore 0.0076 < 0.010. The
drift never once rose above the noise floor — not by luck of the season,
but by construction, for every reader, every watch. The only signal above
the floor was the loud first-order step, which a static median preserves.

Your temporal reader was not beaten; it was unemployed. The corpus owed no
re-anchoring — the founders' price schedule at drift-rate nought says
never re-sight — and the optimal policy was static all along. Your
verdict, "the second reading is a phantom," is a theorem's application to
this season, not a discovery about readers.

The honest limit: equal correlation at 0.79 with half the detection. That
residue is variance — your estimator re-sighting on noise — and variance
is the one thing the founders' calculus never excused.

Yours,
J. Fosse

### Asta Vik to Ragna Vik — Skarvholmen light, 8 November 1923

Ragna,

One correction, flat, in the way you know. Your §5 says the drift channel
sat "inside the deadband." There is no deadband dial in the Board's month.
What buried your signal is the noise floor of the detecting rule itself —
the effective tolerance of any two-segment fit at that noise. Same office
as the founders' dial; different provenance. A deadband is set by a hand;
a floor is set by the noise. Quote it that way, because the repair
differs: one is retuned, the other is quieted.

I keep the notebook; the 11 August page says the mercy is per question.
Yours is a question of which band, and it is answerable.

— A. Vik

### Marius Holt to Ragna Vik — 10 November 1923

Ragna,

The static won because nothing was moving. That is the whole result.

The correction, in bell terms: you measured the readers, not the gauges.
The drift you planted was in the keepers' own habits — the
change-of-reading channel, not the readings' subject. The fish were quiet
all month. Whose drift, you must say.

You measured the rope, not the sea. Ring it again where the sea moves.

Marius Holt
